The Profile Audit

What's keeping you from showing up in search.

3 Things That Decide Your Ranking
  • Match: Google checks if your business name, category, and description match what someone actually typed into the search bar.
  • Distance: Google favors businesses closer to the person searching, so you'll rank higher for nearby customers than far-away ones.
  • Trust: Reviews, photos, and how often you update your profile all signal to Google that you're a real, active business worth showing.

Your dashboard: business.google.com

Step 1: Verification & Category (do these first — nothing else counts until this is right)

Not verified? Go to business.google.com, claim your listing, click "Verify now." Google picks the method: mailed postcard (5–14 days), instant phone/email, or a short video of your storefront.

Category Mistakes to Avoid

Too broad: "General Contractor" instead of "Roofing Contractor" dilutes you — you stop ranking well for the specific job people actually search for.

Primary and secondary mixed up: your primary category should be your single biggest source of revenue, not just your favorite service. Google weighs it more heavily than every secondary category combined.

Copying a competitor's category exactly: it's a fine starting point, but if their listing is thin or wrong, you're just inheriting their mistake. Cross-check it against the table above.

How to Post to Your Google My Business Page
  1. Go to business.google.com and sign in.
  2. Click Posts in the left menu.
  3. Click Add update, pick a type below.
  4. Add your text and a photo, then Publish.
  • What's New: default — no date or deal attached.
  • Offer: adds a coupon code + expiry date, for discounts.
  • Event: adds a date range, for something on a specific day.
  • Product: adds a price + buy button, stays in your Products tab instead of expiring in 7 days.

Your Photo Plan

Which shots to take first, and how to keep adding more.

Don't try to shoot all your photos in one sitting — that's how this task gets skipped entirely. Do 4 now, then trickle the rest in over the following weeks. Steady new photos also tell Google you're an active business, which helps your ranking.

Round 1 · This Week (the essentials)
Round 2 · One New Photo Every Week or Two

Set a phone reminder. Pick whichever's easiest to grab that week — order doesn't matter.

No storefront? If you work out of a van, a client's home, or a job site, use photos of your work vehicle, tools, before/after job shots, and your team instead.

Photo tips: Turn on location services first — when Google can see where a photo was taken, it double-checks that your listed address is real, which helps your ranking. Keep it clear and well-lit — no blur, no clutter.

Templates

Copy, paste into your profile, then fill in the brackets to fit your business.

Business Description
[Business Name] is a [type of business] serving [city/area] and the surrounding communities. We specialize in [core service or product], and what sets us apart is [what makes you different — e.g. same-day service, family-owned, certified/licensed]. Since [year you started], we've helped [type of customer] with [the specific result they came to you for]. [Call to action — e.g. "Call today for a free quote" or "Stop by and see us"].

Keep it under 750 characters — that's the limit Google gives you.

Tips: Put your city and core service in the first sentence — that's what people scan for first. Name one real differentiator, not a generic claim like "quality service." Skip keyword-stuffing; write it for a human, not the algorithm.

Review Reply Starters
Good Review
Thank you, [name]! We're so glad you had a great experience with [specific thing they mentioned]. We look forward to seeing you again.
Good Review (short)
Thanks so much, [name]! Really appreciate you taking the time to write this.
Bad Review
Hi [name], thank you for the feedback. We're sorry to hear about [the issue, restated briefly and without defensiveness] — that's not the experience we want for our customers. Please reach out to us directly at [phone/email] so we can make it right.
Bad Review (no easy fix)
Hi [name], we're sorry your experience didn't meet expectations. We take this seriously and would like to understand what happened — please contact us directly at [phone/email] so we can look into [the issue] and make it right.
Mixed Review
Hi [name], thanks for the honest review! Glad you liked [thing they liked] — and noted on [the complaint], we're working on that. Hope to see you again soon.
Mixed Review (pricing/wait complaint)
Hi [name], thank you for the feedback and for giving us a try. We're glad you liked [thing they liked] — on [price/wait time complaint], that's fair, and we're actively working to improve it. Hope to earn a five-star visit next time.

Reply to every review, good or bad, within a few days — Google counts owner responses as another activity signal, and other customers read them before booking.

Google Post Caption
[What's new — a product, a seasonal offer, an event]. [One sentence on why it matters to the customer]. [Call to action — e.g. "Call now," "Book online," "Stop in this week"].

Google Posts show up directly on your profile and expire after 7 days — a fresh one every week or two signals to Google that you're active.

Example — home service business: "Fall cleanup slots are filling up fast this month. Book now and we'll have your yard ready before the leaves pile up. Call or message us today to grab a spot."

Example — appointment-based business: "New for the holidays: gift cards for any service, in any amount. Perfect for the person on your list who deserves a little pampering. Buy online or stop by the front desk."

Example — restaurant/retail: "Our fall menu is here, featuring pumpkin pancakes and a fresh apple cider. Available all season while supplies last. Come in and try it this week."

Check Your Results

Come back in 2 weeks and confirm it's working.

Where To Look

In your dashboard, click Performance (older accounts: Insights) to see how customers found and used your listing this month.

The 3 Numbers That Matter

What's a healthy number? There's no universal target since it depends on your industry and area. As a rough benchmark, local SEO studies generally find well-optimized profiles convert somewhere around 5–10% of their views into an action — a call, a direction request, or a website click. If you're well under that after a full 2 weeks, it's worth reviewing your photos and description again, not just your numbers.

No movement after 2 weeks? Work through these in order:

  1. Re-check Step 1 — confirm your listing is actually Verified, not just claimed.
  2. Confirm your Primary Category is the closest match, not just a broad guess.
  3. Add 3+ new photos — a stale gallery is one of the biggest silent rank-killers.
  4. Reply to every review since your last check, good or bad.
  5. Post something new (an update, offer, or event) — inactivity reads as "closed" to Google's ranking.

FAQ's

Answers for the situations that come up once you're actually running this.

• Google re-verifying me after an edit?
Changing category or address can trigger video verification. Have your business registration, a utility bill, and your signage ready to film in one take.

• Competitor stuffing keywords in their name?
Don't copy them — Google can suspend for that. Click "Suggest an edit" on their listing to report the real name.

• Work from home, don't want your address public?
Set your profile as a Service Area Business — hide your street address and set a service radius instead.

• Listing suspended out of nowhere?
Usually caused by a recent edit that looked spammy to Google (a keyword-stuffed name, wrong category, or address mismatch). Go to business.google.com, check the notification for the specific reason, resolve that one issue, then file a reinstatement request from the same dashboard. It typically takes a few days to a couple weeks — don't make more edits while it's under review, that resets the clock.

• Someone else already claimed your business?
This happens with listings Google auto-generated years ago. Search for your business on Google Maps, click "Claim this business," and choose "Request access" — Google will prompt the current owner to respond, or let you verify ownership directly if it's been inactive.

• A duplicate listing is competing with yours?
Duplicates split your reviews and confuse Google about which one to rank. Search Maps for your business name and address — if you find a second listing you don't control, click "Suggest an edit" on it and report it as a duplicate of your verified profile.

• Reviews disappeared?
Google periodically removes reviews it flags as fake, off-topic, or posted from a suspicious account — this can happen even to real, legitimate reviews. It's rarely something you did wrong. Give it a few days; if a real review doesn't return, ask that customer to repost it.

Free Bonus

10 Ready-Made Seasonal Captions

One caption for roughly every 5 weeks. Each one comes with the specific Google Business Profile move to pair it with — not just a sentence to post.

1. New Year: New year, new [service/look/menu item]. Kick off [year] with [offer]. [Call to action].Tactic: fresh-start effect — people act on resolutions early in January. Pair with: use the "Offer" post type instead of the default "What's New" — it adds a redeem button and an optional coupon code the plain post type doesn't have.
2. Valentine's / Feb: Treat someone you love this month — [offer or gift idea]. [Call to action].Tactic: gifting occasion narrows the decision to "what," not "whether." Pair with: list the gift item itself as a Product with a price — Products show in their own carousel on your profile, independent of the post's 7-day expiration.
3. Spring: Spring's here, and so is [seasonal service/offer]. [Call to action].Tactic: seasonal-renewal cue. Pair with: swap your profile's cover photo to a current seasonal shot the same week — recently updated photos are one of the "active business" signals Google's ranking weighs.
4. Summer: Beat the heat with [seasonal offer]. [Call to action].Tactic: relief framing for a real pain point (the heat). Pair with: if it's a limited window, use the "Event" post type instead of "Offer" — Event posts display a start/end date range prominently, not just a caption.
5. Back-to-School: Back-to-school season means [relevant offer or reminder]. [Call to action].Tactic: calendar-anchored urgency — everyone's clock resets the same week. Pair with: add the specific item or service to your Products tab with a price — a text-only post never reaches that carousel.
6. Fall / Halloween: Getting into the spirit of the season with [seasonal item or offer]. [Call to action].Tactic: novelty + seasonal FOMO. Pair with: use "What's New" (not Offer/Event) and attach a fresh photo — What's New favors recency, which fits a one-off seasonal moment better than a formal offer.
7. Holiday Season: The holidays are here — [gift cards, holiday hours, or offer]. [Call to action].Tactic: gift urgency plus logistics reassurance in one line. Pair with: update your listed Holiday Hours in the dashboard the same day — a caption implying you're open while your listed hours disagree is one of the fastest ways to earn a 1-star "wrong hours" review.
8. Black Friday / Year-End Sale: [discount]% off this weekend only. [Call to action].Tactic: scarcity plus a hard deadline. Pair with: use the "Offer" post type and set its end date to the sale's last day — it carries a coupon-code field and auto-expires, so it won't sit live showing an expired discount into next week.
9. Local Event: We're proud to [sponsor/take part in] [local event] this month. Stop by and say hi. [Call to action].Tactic: community trust signal — local relevance, not just a promo. Pair with: geotag the accompanying photo at the event's address — location-tagged photos are a factor Google weighs for "near me" ranking, on top of the caption itself.
10. Business Milestone: We just hit [milestone — years in business, customers served]. Thank you, [city]! [Call to action].Tactic: social proof via longevity or volume. Pair with: time this post to go up right after your next 5-star review lands, so a new visitor sees the milestone and fresh praise together instead of as two separate, forgettable updates.

Post one every 1–2 weeks in rough order through the year, swapping in whatever's actually true for your business that month.
